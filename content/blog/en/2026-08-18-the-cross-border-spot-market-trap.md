## The Illusion of Cross-Border Capacity
When supply chains tighten, U.S.-based digital brokers scramble for capacity to cover their enterprise accounts. Their immediate solution is often to reach across the border, contacting Mexican brokers or independent carriers to cover one-off spot loads. On paper, the freight gets moved. In reality, this dynamic creates a deeply unstable supply chain built on transactional band-aids.

This approach treats Mexican carriers as disposable, overflow capacity rather than strategic partners. The moment the market shifts or U.S. domestic capacity loosens, those U.S. brokers vanish, leaving our Mexican colleagues without the consistent volume they need to maintain their operations.

## The Cost of Transactional Relationships
For mid-market shippers, relying on brokers who operate this way introduces massive risk. When your logistics provider has no long-term relationship with the carrier actually hauling your freight south of the border, you have zero guarantee of service quality, equipment maintenance, or security. 

A U.S. broker pushing a quick spot load to an unvetted Mexican carrier cannot guarantee that the truck has the mechanical integrity to survive the 110-degree Sonora heat. They cannot guarantee secure routing or seamless cross-border communication. When an issue inevitably arises, the transactional nature of the relationship means there is no accountability.
