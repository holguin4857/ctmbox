## The Danger of Improper Weight Distribution
In cross-border logistics, a perfectly scheduled shipment can be derailed before it even leaves the loading dock. One of the most common, yet critical, errors made by shipping facilities is improper weight distribution—specifically, loading all the heavy cargo onto the nose (front) of the trailer.

A nose-heavy trailer is not just an inconvenience; it is a severe safety hazard. Pushing too much weight onto the drive axles fundamentally alters the handling of the tractor, compromising steering control and braking distance. Furthermore, it guarantees an immediate Department of Transportation (DOT) axle weight violation the moment the truck pulls onto a weigh station scale. 

## The Cascading Weekend Delay
When a facility improperly loads a trailer, the cascading delays can destroy supply chain velocity. 

Consider a common Friday scenario: A truck is loaded heavily on the nose. The professional driver identifies the hazard and requests a rework to distribute the weight evenly across the tandem axles. However, the facility closes early for the weekend. The driver is instructed to return Saturday morning, only to find the facility unstaffed and unable to assist until Monday. 

Because of a single facility loading error, high-value freight sits idle for an entire weekend. For mid-market shippers trying to hit strict Q3 production schedules, this level of third-party negligence is unacceptable.
